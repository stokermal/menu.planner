Baked Garlic-Herb Chicken Breasts

Our Best Bites, p. 110

30 min · 6, generously servings · 280 cal · 45g protein

Ingredients

  • 3 pounds boneless, skinless chicken breasts
  • 2 tablespoons olive oil
  • 2 teaspoons seasoning salt
  • 4-5 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 teaspoon black pepper
  • Optional: chopped fresh rosemary, oregano, sage, or tarragon

Steps

  1. Optional brine, 15 to 30 minutes: dissolve 1/4 cup kosher salt in 4 cups cold water and submerge the breasts. Salt does not just season — it loosens the muscle proteins so they hold on to water during cooking, which is why brined chicken stays juicy even if you overshoot the temperature slightly. Fifteen minutes helps; thirty is better. Do not go past an hour or the texture turns spongy.
  2. If you brined: rinse the breasts well and pat completely dry, and cut the seasoning salt to 1 teaspoon. The brine already salted the meat, and the full 2 teaspoons on top of it will be too much.
  3. Heat the oven to 425 degrees F.
  4. Pound the thick end of each breast until the whole piece is an even thickness, about 3/4 inch. Put it in a zip bag first and use a rolling pin or a heavy pan. This matters more than anything else on the list — an uneven breast has to overcook at the thin end to finish at the thick end, and that thin overcooked part is what people mean when they say chicken breast is dry.
  5. Pat the surface completely dry. Wet chicken steams instead of browning, and the browning is most of the flavour.
  6. Rub with the olive oil, then the seasoning salt, minced garlic, pepper, and herbs if using.
  7. Arrange on a sheet pan with space between the pieces. Crowded chicken traps steam and stays pale.
  8. Bake 18 to 22 minutes, until an instant-read thermometer in the thickest part reads 160 degrees F. Start checking at 16 minutes. Use a thermometer rather than cutting in to look — every cut lets juice out, and by the time chicken looks done at the centre it has usually gone past.
  9. Rest 5 to 10 minutes before slicing. The temperature carries up to 165 as it sits, so 160 out of the oven is done, not underdone. Cutting straight away spills the juice onto the board instead of leaving it in the meat.
  10. Slice against the grain — across the lines running through the meat, not along them. Shorter fibres eat more tender.
